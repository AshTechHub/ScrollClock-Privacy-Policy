ScrollClock Privacy Policy
Last updated: 16 November 2026
ScrollClock (“the app”) is an Android app published by AshMade (“I”, “me”). It counts the Instagram Reels you scroll through each day and shows you the number, together with the time you spend on Reels. It also offers streaks, weekly recaps, home-screen widgets, reminders, and an Explore tab with simple things to do instead of scrolling. This policy explains what the app does with information.
Contact: appsbyashtech@gmail.com
The short version
• Your reel counts, time spent, streaks, Explore progress, settings and mascot picture stay on your phone. We run no servers for the app and cannot see any of it.
• There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no third-party services, apart from Google Play’s own billing library, which handles the optional one-time purchase.
• The app does not send your information to us or to anyone else. Information leaves your phone only when you choose to send it yourself: an email report, a picture you share, or a data file you export.
• The only time the app itself contacts the internet is through Google Play’s billing library for the optional purchase. It talks to Google Play, not to us. This is why the app lists the internet and network-status permissions. The app also lists the vibration permission, used only for the optional milestone vibration.
• ScrollClock uses Android’s Accessibility service, which is off until you switch it on yourself. You can switch it off at any time with one tap, for example before you pay with a payment app (see “Payment and banking apps”).
• The app is excluded from Android’s backup. You can move your history to a new phone with Export and Import.
• Old data is cleaned up automatically, you can erase your counts inside the app at any time, and uninstalling removes everything.
What the app does
All of the following happens on your phone:
• It counts the reels you swipe through in Instagram, measures how long the Reels screen is open, and shows a floating counter while you scroll.
• It shows your day, week, month and year, your peak hours, your levels and streaks, and a weekly recap.
• It can show milestone messages (and vibrate briefly), remind you to take a break, and remind you to wind down in the evening, using only your phone’s clock.
• It lets you pause counting for a time you choose.
• The Explore tab offers ideas, short guided exercises, timers and a few small games, and remembers your progress.
• It offers home-screen widgets and a Quick Settings tile.
• It lets you share a picture, export and import your data, and (as an optional purchase) use your own mascot picture.
How the app uses Android’s Accessibility service
ScrollClock uses Android’s Accessibility service for one purpose only: to notice when you swipe to the next reel in Instagram, so it can count it, measure how long the Reels screen is open, and show the floating counter. It is not used for anything else, and the app never performs actions on your behalf.
While Instagram is open, Android tells the app:
• which screen is showing,
• the technical names and positions of the on-screen elements that are visible, and
• when the Reels feed scrolls.
The app does not read the text on your screen. It does not read your messages, comments, posts, usernames or passwords, and it does not take screenshots or record your screen. Android’s Accessibility permission is broad and would technically allow an app to do those things, but ScrollClock does not use it that way.
Other apps. Android also tells the app when windows of other apps appear. The app ignores what is in them. It only checks which app has the focus, so it can hide the floating counter when you leave Instagram, and it keeps no record of which apps you use. So that a keyboard does not hide the counter, the app also looks at which keyboard apps are enabled on your phone; this list is not stored or sent anywhere. To know whether ScrollClock itself is switched on, the app reads Android’s list of enabled accessibility services and looks only for its own entry.
Your agreement. The Accessibility service is off until you switch it on yourself in Android settings. Before that, the app shows a notice that explains the points above, and you must tap “Agree and continue”. If you tap “Deny”, nothing is turned on. You can switch the service off at any time, in Android’s Accessibility settings or from inside the app, and counting stops immediately. After you switch it off, the app remembers your agreement for 30 days, so that you can switch it back on (for example after a payment) without reading the notice again. If ScrollClock stays off for more than 30 days, the app deletes that record and asks for your agreement again the next time you enable the counter.
Switching off and on. When you ask it to (with a button, the Quick Settings tile or an app shortcut), the app can switch its own Accessibility service off. It can never switch it on: Android only allows you to do that, in Accessibility settings.
Payment and banking apps
Some payment and banking apps (UPI apps included) check whether any accessibility service is switched on, and may warn you, limit what you can do, or refuse to open. That is a security rule of those apps. It applies to every app that uses Android’s Accessibility service, not only ScrollClock.
• ScrollClock does not hide from those checks and does not try to.
• ScrollClock does not read, record or interact with the screens of your payment apps, and it keeps no record of which payment apps you use.
• To switch the counter off before you pay: tap “Paying with a payment app?” on the Today tab, use “Payment apps” in the Help tab, use the ScrollClock Quick Settings tile (you add it yourself), long-press the app icon and choose “Counter off”, or switch it off in Android’s Accessibility settings. Nothing is counted while it is off.
• To switch it on again, use “Turn counter on” in the app, the tile, or the “Counter on” shortcut. Android then opens Accessibility settings, where you switch ScrollClock on.
• The tile and the shortcuts only switch the counter off and open Android’s settings. They collect nothing. The tile checks whether the counter is on so that it can show “Counting” or “Off”.
On newer versions of Android, Advanced Protection mode may stop apps like ScrollClock from using the Accessibility service. That is Android’s rule and the app cannot change it.
Information stored on your device
The app stores the following on your phone, in its private storage. We cannot see it.
• Days the counter was running: a list of dates (no times), used to work out your streaks of days with few reels.
• Your daily reel counts: a date and a number.
• Reels per hour: a date, an hour and a number, for roughly the last month, used for your peak hours. Older hourly detail is deleted automatically.
• Time spent on Reels: how long the Reels screen was open each day. If you stop swiping for more than three minutes, the extra time is not counted.
• Explore progress: how many things you finished each day and the minutes they took, which ones you finished (both kept for two weeks, then deleted automatically), which task you accepted today and are still doing, and your best score in each game.
• Pause: the time your pause ends and the last pause length you chose.
• Troubleshooting information: a few timestamps (such as when the service started, when Instagram was last seen, when the Reels screen was last recognised and when the last reel was counted). If the app cannot recognise the Reels screen, it may also keep a short list of the technical names of reel-related screen elements it noticed and the Instagram version that was installed when the Reels screen was last recognised. This powers the Status and “Reel Counter Disappeared?” help in the Help tab.
• Settings and small bits of state: for example whether milestone messages are on, whether vibration on milestones is on, whether the break reminder and the evening wind-down are on and their times, your chosen pill color, whether you closed the welcome card, whether first-time setup is done, and the date, count and wording of the last milestone message shown (so the same one is not repeated straight away).
• Your agreement to the Accessibility notice and, while the counter is off, the time you switched it off (kept for up to 30 days, as described above).
• Whether the mascot unlock is active: a simple yes or no, so the app does not have to wait for the store each time.
• A custom mascot picture, if you choose one: the picture is copied into the app’s private storage, made smaller, and any editing (such as removing the background) happens on your phone. The app can only read the single picture or file you pick in Android’s photo picker, and the picture never leaves your phone.
• Temporary files: a picture you share, or a data file you export, is first made in the app’s temporary storage. The app deletes these files automatically after about a day.
The messages the app shows (milestone banners on the floating counter, the short line under your time spent, the streak messages on the Stats page, and the ideas and tips in the Explore tab) are chosen from text built into the app, based only on your counts, time, streaks and the time of day. No information about you leaves your phone to produce them. When a milestone message appears, the app can also vibrate your phone briefly. Whether it vibrates, and how many taps, depends only on your count. No information leaves your phone to do this.
Automatic clean-up
About once a day, ScrollClock tidies up after itself:
• Explore progress older than two weeks is deleted (the app only ever shows the last seven days).
• Hourly detail older than about a month is deleted.
• Temporary files made for sharing and exporting are deleted after about a day.
• If the counter has been off for more than 30 days, the record of your agreement is deleted.
Your daily counts and your best game scores are kept until you reset or uninstall, because your stats, levels and streaks are built from them.
Home-screen widgets
If you add a ScrollClock widget to your home screen, it shows today’s count, your level and streak, or the last seven days. ScrollClock draws it on your phone from the counts stored there, and your launcher app displays it. Nothing is sent anywhere. Widgets refresh when your counts change and roughly every 30 minutes. Remove the widget to stop it.
Sharing, exporting and importing
These only happen when you tap the button for them.
• Share a picture (your streak or your weekly recap). ScrollClock draws a picture on your phone: the app name, the number of days or reels, the dates of the week with a bar for each day, and a short line of text. It hands the picture to Android’s share sheet, and it goes only to the app you pick. That app’s own privacy policy applies to what it does with it. We do not receive it.
• Export my data (CSV). ScrollClock makes two small files: one row per day (date, reels, minutes on Reels, and whether the counter was running) and reels per hour for the last month. They are handed to Android’s share sheet in the same way. The files are plain text and are not encrypted, and they show when you were scrolling, so share them only with people and apps you trust.
• Import my data (CSV). You pick the files with Android’s file picker. The app can read only the files you pick (up to 2 MB each), checks every row, and adds the history to your phone without replacing larger numbers that are already there. Nothing is uploaded.
Copies of pictures and files that you sent to other apps or saved elsewhere stay where you put them, and you can delete them yourself.
The optional mascot purchase (Google Play)
Using your own mascot picture is an optional one-time purchase made through Google Play.
• Google Play handles the payment. Your card or payment details never reach us, and Google’s own terms and privacy policy apply to the purchase.
• To do this, the app uses Google Play’s billing library, which needs an internet connection. The app uses it to show the price, start the purchase, and check what your Google account has bought. It checks again when the app opens, so a refund removes the unlock. It also does this after you reinstall the app, or when you tap “Restore purchase”.
• We have no accounts. Your purchase is tied to your Google account in Google Play, not to us.
• Google may give us limited order information in Play Console (for example the order number, the product, the date and the country). We use it only to handle purchases, refunds and support.
Permissions the app uses
• Accessibility service (you switch it on yourself): to count reels, as described above.
• Vibrate: for the optional milestone vibration, and for nothing else.
• Internet and network state: used only by Google Play’s billing library for the optional purchase.
• Quick Settings tile (only if you add the tile): lets Android show the ScrollClock tile. It gives the app no access to your information.
• Seeing whether Instagram is installed: used to check that Instagram is there and, in the optional support report, to show its version.
The app does not ask for, and cannot use, your location, contacts, camera, microphone, calls, messages or calendar. It can read only a single picture or file that you choose yourself in Android’s pickers. When you tap “Privacy policy” in the app, your browser (not ScrollClock) opens this policy’s web page, which is hosted on GitHub Pages.
Information we receive
We receive nothing automatically. The only exception is the optional “Email report” button in the Help tab. If you tap it, the app opens your own email app with a report already written. The report starts with a space where you can describe the problem, then an automatic part containing your phone’s manufacturer and model, your Android version (API level), the ScrollClock version, your Instagram version (the app checks whether Instagram is installed, and which version, only for this report), whether the service is on, the timing information described above, and the technical names of Instagram screen elements. The automatic part does not include your reel counts. What you type in the description is up to you, so please do not include personal information. Nothing is sent unless you press Send in your email app. You can read and edit the whole report first.
If you send it, we receive the report and your email address, plus anything else you write. We use them only to look into counting problems and to reply to you, and we keep them for up to 12 months, or delete them sooner on request. Anything else you choose to send us yourself, such as a picture or file you share with us, is used only to reply to you.
Who we share information with
We do not sell or share your information. The app contains no advertising or analytics code and no third-party SDKs other than Google Play’s billing library described above. If you email us a report, it passes through your email provider and ours, as any email does. If you share a picture or file, it goes to the app you choose, as described above.
Backup and reinstalling
ScrollClock is excluded from Android’s backup. If you reinstall the app or set up a new phone, your counts, settings, Explore progress, best scores and mascot picture will not be restored, and the Accessibility notice will be shown again. To keep your history, tap “Export my data (CSV)” before you switch phones and “Import my data (CSV)” afterwards. The export holds your counts, times and counter days, but not your settings, game scores or mascot picture. If you bought the mascot unlock, it comes back automatically when the app checks with Google Play, or when you tap “Restore purchase” in the Premium tab.
Your choices and deleting your data
• Switch the Accessibility service off at any time: in Android’s Accessibility settings, or with one tap in the app, the Quick Settings tile or the app-icon shortcut. This stops all counting immediately and is how you withdraw your agreement. The record of your agreement is deleted after 30 days off, or when you uninstall the app or clear its storage.
• Tap “Reset all counts” in the Help tab to erase your saved reel counts, time spent, streak history, peak hours, weekly recaps and your Explore progress (things finished and tasks in progress). Your best game scores, your settings, your mascot picture and your purchase are kept.
• Tap “Use default” in the Premium tab to remove your custom mascot picture.
• Turn off “Vibrate on milestones” (or “Milestone messages”) in the Stats tab under Settings, and turn off the break reminder and the wind-down reminder where you set them.
• Delete files you exported or pictures you shared from wherever you saved or sent them.
• Uninstall the app, or clear its storage in Android settings, to remove all of its data from your phone.
• Your purchase record is held by Google Play. You can manage it through your Google account.
• Because we hold no personal data from the app itself, there is nothing for us to export or delete from it. If you have emailed us and want a copy of what you sent, or want it deleted, write to appsbyashtech@gmail.com. We will reply within 30 days. Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR or UK GDPR, the CCPA/CPRA, or India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023), you may also have legal rights over your personal information, such as to access, correct or delete it, to withdraw consent, and to complain to your local data protection authority. We will honor them for any information we hold.
Security
Your data is stored in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read on a normal Android phone. Because the app does not send your data anywhere, there is no server for us to protect. Files you export are plain text and leave that protection once you save or send them, so look after them. The app shortcut that switches the counter off can be started only by Android’s launcher, and the tile only by Android itself.
